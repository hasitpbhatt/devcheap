# Newsletter Pitches — awesome-ai-apps

Three tailored pitches for The Rundown, Ben's Bites, and TLDR AI. Each includes a subject line, the pitch, and why it fits that newsletter's audience.

Facts that apply to all three: **127 AI apps, 10 categories, curated, verified links, alphabetical within categories, MIT-licensed, open to PR contributions.**

---

## 1. The Rundown

**Subject line:** A curated list of 127 AI apps that actually stand out (no wrappers)

**Pitch:**

> Most "best AI apps" roundups are bloated lists of hundreds of tools, most of them dead links or throwaway wrappers. Our new resource, awesome-ai-apps, is the opposite: a curated, deliberately lean collection of just **127 AI apps across 10 categories** — AI Chatbots, AI Coding, AI Image, AI Video, AI Audio & Voice, AI Productivity, AI Automation & Agents, and more — with every link verified and the list updated regularly.
>
> The rule is simple: only apps that genuinely stand out get in. It covers the household names your readers already know (ChatGPT, Claude, Midjourney, Notion AI, n8n) but also the ones that win on substance — Cursor and Devin in coding, NotebookLM in productivity, Ollama and OpenHands for local/self-hosted. Alphabetical, scannable, zero fluff, MIT-licensed and open to community PRs.

**Why it fits The Rundown:** Your readers want one trustworthy link they can act on, not 40. A lean, verified, category-organized list is a natural fit for a daily AI update and is highly shareable. Suggested framing: a short item under "AI Tools" pointing to the repo as *the* list to bookmark.

## 2. Ben's Bites

**Subject line:** 127 genuinely good AI apps, curated and verified — including the dev sections

**Pitch:**

> AI lists usually fail devs in two ways: they're bloated with wrappers, and they bury the tools that actually matter for building. awesome-ai-apps fixes both. It's a curated list of **127 AI apps across 10 categories** with verified links, kept lean on purpose — only apps that genuinely stand out.
>
> For your dev-heavy audience, the strongest sections are AI Coding (GitHub Copilot, Cursor, Claude Code, Windsurf, Devin, Bolt.new, plus open-source agents like Aider, Cline, and Roo Code) and Local & Open Source (Ollama, Open WebUI, Jan, LibreChat, OpenHands). There's also AI Automation & Agents (n8n, Zapier, Make, Dify, LangGraph) and dev APIs in audio (Deepgram, AssemblyAI) — all alphabetical, all link-verified, MIT-licensed and open to PRs.

**Why it fits Ben's Bites:** This is a dev-flavored, "skip the fluff" resource — exactly the tone of your curation. The open-source and automation sections give it legs as a repeat bookmark rather than a one-day link. Suggested framing: a link roundup item titled "The anti-bloat AI list."

## 3. TLDR AI

**Subject line:** The anti-fluff AI list: 127 apps, 10 categories, verified links

**Pitch:**

> TLDR, this is a list for people who don't read lists. awesome-ai-apps is a curated collection of **127 AI apps across 10 categories**, kept deliberately lean — only apps that genuinely stand out get in. Every link is verified, entries are alphabetical for fast scanning, and the list is updated regularly.
>
> It spans AI Chatbots, AI Coding, AI Search, AI Writing, AI Image, AI Video, AI Audio & Voice, AI Productivity, AI Automation & Agents, and Local & Open Source. Coverage runs from ChatGPT, Perplexity, and NotebookLM to Sora, ElevenLabs, Suno, and open-source tools like Ollama and OpenHands. MIT-licensed and open to contributions — found a better app? PR it.

**Why it fits TLDR AI:** It's a curated, low-noise resource with a clear TLDR ("127 apps, 10 categories, verified") that survives the "save for later" test better than a 500-item list. Suggested framing: a one-liner in the AI tools section with a single strong stat and the repo link.
