# Hacker News — Prepared Replies to Likely Criticism

Five of the most likely comments on a Show HN for an awesome list, with thoughtful, non-defensive replies. Default stance: **agree, add honest nuance, never argue into a corner.**

---

## 1. "Why another awesome list? There are already a hundred of these."

**Reply:**

Honest answer: because most of them are link dumps that go stale, and I kept running into them when I wanted a working answer. The average "top AI tools" article is SEO filler; the average awesome list is a repo that stopped being touched a year ago.

Two things I'm deliberately doing differently:
1. **Lean on purpose.** 127 apps across 10 categories, each one held to "genuinely stands out" rather than "exists." A person can scan the whole thing in ten minutes. A 5,000-tool directory that nobody can navigate isn't a better resource, it's a different (worse) problem.
2. **Verified links, actively maintained.** Links are checked when entries land, dead links get fixed when reported, and there's a real contribution path so it doesn't rot.

If a better-maintained list exists, I'd genuinely like the link — I'd rather point people to the best one than compete for the sake of it.

---

## 2. "How is this different from [Awesome-GPTs / awesome-ai / a specific directory]?"

**Reply:**

Depends on which one you mean, but the core differences are consistent:
- **It's narrow but current.** Many awesome lists in this space are either per-model (only GPT wrappers) or huge and largely unmaintained. This one is category-wide (chatbots through local/open-source) but small enough to actually maintain — 127 entries is a number one person can keep honest.
- **The bar is explicit.** Entries need a working link, an honest one-liner, and genuine differentiation. That filters out the vaporware and the "wrapper with a landing page" apps that pad out bigger lists.
- **It's a living repo, not a static page.** Open contribution, issue templates for dead links, and git history as the update log.

I'm not claiming it's better than every list out there — it's built to fill the specific gap of "small, current, verified."

---

## 3. "Lists go stale in six months. How do I know these links work?"

**Reply:**

You can't *know* — and I won't pretend otherwise. What I can say is how it's handled:

- Links are verified before any entry merges.
- There's a bug-report template in the repo, so flagging a dead link is a two-minute job, and reported ones get fixed fast.
- The git history is the proof of upkeep: you can see when entries were added and when fixes landed, rather than trusting a "last updated" claim.

It's a best-effort living document, and being honest about that is exactly why I'd rather have a small list I can keep current than a big one that quietly rots. And if you find a dead link right now — file it and I'll fix it today. That's the system working.

---

## 4. "Selection is subjective. Why should I trust your picks? Are there affiliate links?"

**Reply:**

On trust: I'm not asking you to — the list is a starting point, and the one-liners tell you what each tool is so you can judge for yourself in seconds. If a pick is wrong, that's a legit contribution: the repo takes PRs, and disagreements get resolved by improving the entry, not by arguing.

On money: no affiliate links, no paid placement, nothing to sell. If I were monetizing, the incentives would be wrong and the curation would drift — the whole reason the list is worth using is that it isn't. The only thing I'm building is a resource that's useful enough that people contribute to it.

And on subjectivity, one honest caveat: quality calls are judgment calls. But "curated by a human who has a bar and is accountable in public via git history" beats "algorithmically scraped" for most people most of the time.

---

## 5. "127 apps is nothing. [Directory] has 10,000+ tools. Why should I use yours?"

**Reply:**

Because you're not going to read 10,000 tools. The question almost everyone actually has is "what do I use for X?" — and a 10,000-entry directory answers that with a search bar and an ad, not a recommendation.

I'm not trying to be the biggest index. The bar is: can a person scan this in ten minutes and find the right tool for their use case? That works at 127 carefully-chosen entries across 10 categories, and it stops working somewhere around 1,000.

The big directories have real value too — they're comprehensive, which is great when you know roughly what you're looking for. My niche is the opposite: verified, curated, small enough to maintain honestly. Different tools for different jobs.
