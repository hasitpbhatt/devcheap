How we rate a deal

Every deal on this site carries a 1–10 rating. Here is the exact rubric that produces it — six factors, fixed weights, no adjustment for whether we get paid. The same script computes every score, so two people cannot argue about taste.

The six factors

1. Dollar value

25% weight

How much money the offer actually saves you. The biggest number in the offer text sets the baseline, then modifiers apply: unlimited or permanent free tiers add points, and an explicit "saves $X/month" comparison adds more.

$0–$10 → +1 · $10–$50 → +1.5 · $50–$100 → +2 · $100–$200 → +2.5 · $200–$500 → +3 · $500–$1K → +3.5 · $1K–$2K → +4 · $5K+ → +5 · permanent free tier → +2–3

2. Longevity

20% weight

How long the deal lasts. A free tier with no expiry date is worth more than a trial that ends next week, because you can build on it.

free + no expiry → 9 · free + expiry → 7 · lifetime + no expiry → 8 · lifetime + expiry → 6 · trial → 4 · paid → 3

3. Developer relevance

20% weight

Whether this tool sits in a real developer's stack. Core categories — Hosting & Cloud, Database, Monitoring, CI/CD, Testing & QA, Storage & Cloud, Auth, Developer Tools — score highest. Adjacent categories like Productivity and Design score lower but still count.

core tag or category → 8–9 · adjacent category → 6 · off-stack (Social Media, Design) → 3–6

4. Audience breadth

15% weight

How many developers can use it. A free tier anyone can claim beats a program that needs a signed contract, even when the numbers are bigger.

recommended → 8 · neutral → 5 · limited-audience / startup-only / enterprise → 2

5. Signup friction

10% weight

What stands between you and the free tier. No credit card and no account beats a card requirement. Phone verification and broken signups score near the bottom.

no card / no account → 9 · automatic link → 8 · coupon code → 6 · card required → 3 · phone verification → 2 · known broken → 1

6. Ecosystem maturity

10% weight

How likely the tool is to still exist in a year. Category leaders score highest. Affiliate partners get a small bump because a company paying a commission has revenue to keep the product alive — this is the only factor that knows about money, and it moves a score by at most one point.

category leader → 9 · affiliate partner → 7 · everything else → 6

What the score does not know

FactorWhy it is not in the score
Whether we get paidAffiliate status moves ecosystem maturity by at most one point. It cannot push a deal over the featured threshold.
How pretty the landing page isNot a factor. A great deal behind a bad signup flow still gets a low friction score.
How much traffic the vendor gets from usNot a factor, and not tracked per vendor beyond the click count.
Whether the founder is famousNot a factor. No personal names appear in this rubric.

Every deal page shows the factor breakdown next to the score, so you can see exactly where the number came from. If a factor looks wrong, the transparency ledger explains who can change it and how.
